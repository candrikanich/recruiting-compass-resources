The "full ride" is the most misunderstood idea in recruiting. For most families, the real offer is a **partial scholarship** stacked with other aid. Understanding the mechanics keeps your expectations — and your budget — grounded.

## Headcount vs. Equivalency Sports

This single distinction explains almost everything.

- **Headcount sports** (e.g., FBS football, D1 basketball): every scholarship is a full scholarship. You're on or off the list.
- **Equivalency sports** (most others — soccer, baseball, track, swimming): coaches get a *budget* of scholarships to divide. A roster of 30 might split 10 scholarships into partials.

If your sport is equivalency-based, a partial offer isn't a slight — it's the norm.

## What a Partial Offer Covers

A coach might offer 40% of tuition. That percentage can apply to tuition, fees, room, board, and books. Always ask **what the percentage is calculated against**.

## Stacking Other Aid

Athletic money rarely stands alone. Families combine it with:

1. **Academic scholarships** — often larger than the athletic piece.
2. **Need-based aid** — file the FAFSA regardless of income.
3. **Division III** — no athletic scholarships at all, but strong academic and need-based packages.

## Questions to Ask a Coach

- Is this offer renewable each year, or one-time?
- What's expected of my athlete to keep it?
- Can it be combined with academic aid here?

## Next Step

Get any offer **in writing** and compare total cost of attendance across schools — not just the scholarship percentage.
